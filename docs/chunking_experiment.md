## Method

- Corpus: 5 documents (11,739 words total)
- Gold set: 20 hand-labelled questions in `eval/golden.json`
- Grid: 3 strategies x 5 sizes [400, 600, 800, 1000, 1200] x 3 overlaps [0.0, 0.1, 0.15] = 45 configs
- Metric: Recall@5, retrieval only. No LLM, no model-as-judge.
- Hit condition: a top-5 chunk from the correct `source_id` contains the
  verbatim `answer_span`.
- Cap: every chunk re-split until its MiniLM token length is <= 256
  word-pieces; `violations` counts fragments still unsplittable after that.
- Index: exact brute-force cosine in numpy — no ChromaDB, no HNSW
  approximation, so recall measures chunking rather than the index.

## Reading these numbers

- **Recall@5 may saturate near 1.0 for every strategy.** This corpus is
  small and the questions are single-fact lookups, so the gold chunk is
  usually the obvious neighbour. A flat grid is a real result, and it is
  *not* evidence that chunking does not matter — it is evidence that this
  corpus is too easy to discriminate between chunkers. The tie-break then
  does the real work, and "any of these work, take the cheapest" is a
  legitimate outcome.
- **The cap is the filter that actually matters.** A config whose `max_tok`
  exceeds 256 would truncate silently at embedding time and answer from
  the wrong half of a passage. That failure mode is invisible in Recall@5
  and catastrophic in production, which is why it is asserted in a test and
  why cap-violating configs are recorded as skipped rather than scored.
